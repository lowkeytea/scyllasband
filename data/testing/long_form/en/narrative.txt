At first light, Mara unlocked the small observatory above the harbour and opened every shutter. The storm had passed during the night, leaving bright puddles on the road and a clean line between sea and sky. She checked the brass clock, wrote the temperature in a faded ledger, and carried two cups of tea to the eastern window.

By eight, the town was moving again. A baker raised his awning, a bicycle bell answered the gulls, and the first ferry eased away from the pier. Mara had watched these ordinary events for years, yet the calm sequence never felt automatic. Each sound arrived in its own place, and together they made the morning feel complete.

Before leaving, she turned off the lamp, secured the door, and paused on the stairs. The weather would change again, but for a little while everything below her was clear.
